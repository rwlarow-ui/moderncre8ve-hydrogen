## 5. Content Additions to Recommend

### 5a. New Section: "Products to Avoid on Solid Wood Furniture"

**Targets query:** "can i use clorox wipes on wood furniture" (and related "what not to use on wood furniture" queries)

**Recommended H2:** `Products to Avoid on Solid Wood Furniture`

**Content outline:**
- Open with the direct answer: No, do not use Clorox wipes, Lysol wipes, or other chemical disinfectant wipes on solid wood. They contain bleach and harsh chemicals that strip natural oils, damage finishes, and can cause discoloration.
- List of products to avoid: Clorox/Lysol wipes, ammonia-based cleaners (Windex), abrasive scouring pads, silicone-based polishes (Pledge), vinegar (on finished wood), acetone/nail polish remover.
- For each, briefly explain *why* it damages wood (strips oils, clouds finish, raises grain, etc.).
- What to use instead: damp microfiber cloth, mild dish soap solution, and [Lareaux's natural furniture wax] for ongoing protection.
- This section has strong FAQ schema potential (see 5c below).

### 5b. New Section: "Best Finish for a Dining Table Top"

**Targets query:** "best varnish for table top" (and related "best finish for wood table", "how to finish a dining table" queries)

**Recommended H2:** `Best Finish for a Dining Table Top`

**Content outline:**
- Compare the main finish types for dining tables: oil-based polyurethane, water-based polyurethane, Danish oil, tung oil, lacquer, wax.
- Pros and cons of each in the context of daily dining use (food safety, heat resistance, repairability, sheen).
- ModernCre8ve's recommendation: what finishes they use on their own [dining tables] and why.
- Maintenance tips specific to each finish type.
- Mention that a natural wax topcoat (like [Lareaux's natural furniture wax]) extends the life of any finish.
- Include a simple comparison table for quick scanning.
